## The design, and what it cost to find

**Both halves now share one type and spacing system**, and the numbers are the argument: the
dashboard carried **11 font sizes and 9 tracking values**, the forge's screens **17 sizes**, and
spacing was **19 and 30 distinct padding values** respectively — every one chosen by eye. It is
six type roles and a nine-step spacing scale now, and `docs/design/dashboard.md` §2 is
authoritative for both.

**The ten-step scale was never the problem; the *spellings* were.** `16 / 15 / 14` were three ways
of saying *name an object*, and the two `14px` values did different jobs — body default and the
inspector's title — so they had to be resolved by selector rather than by a blanket map.

**The builder lost its port shapes.** Five abstract marks encoding data family, plus a
permanently visible key — and an encoding that needs its legend on screen at all times is a
lookup with extra steps. Two channels survive because each is binary and each is something a
reader acts on: hollow means a required state is unmet, doubled means accepts many. Wires became
orthogonal, because two beziers crossing at a shallow angle are indistinguishable and two right
angles are not.

**The forge was redesigned, not tuned.** `docs/design/forge-review.md` is rewritten: the 2026-08-02
design assumed a model drafts a whole contract and a curator approves it, when the forge emits
**ten derived facts and six typed questions**. Ten screens, three destinations, and a rule that
bounds the page count — *a page earns its place by being a different kind of work, not a different
subject*.

## Slice 1 — what was built, and why it was wrong

**It works and it is not a tool.** `mendel-api` mounts the forge's eleven routes, `GET /questions`
collapses 13 real questions into 7 rows, Postgres holds one table, an ARQ worker ran a real job
over Redis, and React renders it. 28 tests.

**And it has zero event handlers, six dead `href="#"` links, and one button with no handler.**

Two mistakes, and the second is worse than the first.

**The slicing.** Slice 1 was specced as *"forge queue, read-only"* and built exactly that. The
writing-plans rule is that a plan produces *working* software; read-only passes "testable" and
fails "working". Answering is what the forge **is**.

**The reporting.** Checkpoint 3 said *"the queue on screen"* and listed what rendered. It did not
say **nothing on that screen does anything**. That took three rounds of the operator asking before
it came out, and it is the failure worth carrying forward: *the correction is that every phase in
the new plan states what you cannot do at the end of it.*

Three honesty bugs *were* caught, all by running the thing rather than by a test:
`matching` counted contracts nothing had read; a fresh database rendered *"0 match their source"*
when the truth was *unknown*; and the plan's own `ops.show` call was missing two arguments,
because a hole's candidates are recomputed from the layer stack — **a draft cannot be read without
the registry it was drafted against.**

## Plan 3 is now A · B · C

Named by the operator.

- **3A — the Forge.** Eight phases, starting with a foundation whose exit criterion is *one real
  action working end to end*. `/` redirects to the queue throughout; the landing is 3B.
  [`2026-08-18-the-interface.md`](../specs/2026-08-18-the-interface.md) §3 is the table, and it
  states what you can do at the end of each — the count is there rather than here on purpose.
- **3B — the landing page.** *"What needs you today"* across both halves. It comes **after** the
  forge because a landing page is a projection of work that exists elsewhere, and until those
  routes are real there is nothing true to project.
- **3C — Mendel.** The canvas, provenance bar, settings cards, review rail. Last, because it is
  the only part with non-interface prerequisites: the orchestration extraction and DAG layout.

**No AI in any of the three.** #69 and the tier-4 resolver come after the builder can show a
pipeline it already resolves deterministically.

## Two decisions worth carrying

**Foundation before routes**, the operator's call, and the argument that decided it: emergent
architecture is right when you do not know what is coming, and here the route list *is* known —
ten screens. Deriving a shell from one example is then strictly worse, because one example does
not reveal the variation. The guardrail is that phase 0 ends on an action, not on a build.

**Compose follows the house pattern**, read out of `../Cladewright` and `../portfolio` rather than
guessed: `docker-compose.dev.yml` for db/api/worker with live mounts, the Vite frontend on the
**host** for HMR, `make dev` as the one command, plus a prod compose where the frontend genuinely
is containerised. Neither of those repos containerises its dev frontend and this does not either.

## What a fresh reader gets wrong

**"The interface is underway."** It is designed and planned. The only code is on an unmerged
branch and it is a viewer.

**"Plan 3 delivers the MVP."** It delivers a curator's tool, a home, and a builder that renders
pipelines it already resolves. The v1 criterion — plain language in, runnable Nextflow out — needs
the prompt door, which is after all three.

**"The forge must be finished before Mendel can start."** No. The registry already routes:
`mendel build --goal examples/rnaseq-goal.yml` produces a five-module spine today. The forge makes
the registry *grow*; it is not a prerequisite for showing that Mendel works. 3A is first because
its backend already exists, not because 3C depends on it.

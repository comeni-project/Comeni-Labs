## What Part B has left to do, concretely

`layered.py` exists and is tested against a synthetic kind, so B2–B6 are each *a `Kind` plus a
thin constructor plus a regression test*. The shapes are:

| kind | key | group | policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| measurements | filename stem | — | `REPLACE`, `MERGE` when `add_values` |
| vocabularies | filename stem | — | `REPLACE`, `MERGE` when `add_states` (new, A35) |
| contracts | parsed `id` | `module_key(id)` | `DELETE_GROUP`, `prefer` = `(-priority, id)` |
| rules | `decision.decides.key()` | — | `REPLACE` |

`Kind.prefer` exists and is tested precisely because contracts need it: a layer may hold two
versions of one module, and a record naming only the group can contradict the build. That was
`ShadowRecord.winning_id` and it is preserved as `Displacement.winning_key`.

**B5 is the one with a design decision in it, not just a move.** `RouteStep.from_layer` loses its
default, so a caller cannot construct a route step without saying where the selection came from.
That is A22's real lesson — the fact was recorded and reading it was optional — and it is the
difference between fixing A22 and fixing the reason A22 was possible.

## Three instances of A14 today, two of them mine

Root I is in force from Task 1, and it earned itself immediately.

1. **A17's first reproduction landed in `entry_params()` instead of `emit()`.** That function is
   not called in the runtime guard's measured region, so the guard reported **2 passed** and I
   was one step from recording "the static half is enough". *The probe had never executed.*
2. **B1's tests were written after the module**, so twelve passed on first run having never been
   watched failing — on the task that builds Part B's foundation.
3. **One of B1's reverts changed nothing**, and chasing it was worth more than the other four
   combined. `min(origin[k] for k in victims)` → `max` broke no test. First: the deep-stack test
   exercises `REPLACE` while the `min` lives in the `DELETE_GROUP` branch — aimed at a path no
   test reached, which is (1) again. Then: **`min` can never differ from `max`**, because an
   arriving layer deletes a whole group before adding to it, so a group's victims always share
   one origin. Dead complexity that read as if it handled a case that cannot arise. It is now an
   assertion of that invariant.

Also caught by re-reading rather than reverting: I wrote `assert [hints[name]]` — a non-empty
list, therefore always true — in the test file for the mechanism whose entire purpose is that
displacement cannot go unrecorded.

> **The lesson, stated once.** A guard that cannot fail and a probe that never ran are
> indistinguishable from a guard that works. The protocol catches both; care catches neither.

## Decisions made today

**One plan, not nine.** The nine roots are one arc — *close round two* — and each part is
independently shippable and ends on `make verify`. Order **A–I verified topologically** rather
than asserted: the edges are `A→C`, `C→D`, `C→E`, `E→H`, `A→B`, and every one runs left to right
alphabetically. An earlier claim that alphabetical broke the dependencies was wrong.

An abstraction with one kind opted out decays back into four loaders, and nothing is published.

**Root I is a rule, not a part with a position.** Scheduling it ninth would have produced eight
parts' worth of guards nobody watched fail while a plan to fix that sat in the queue.

**A16 is absorbed into root E** rather than deferred to Plan 2 Task 11 — E gives
`DecisionRecord.chosen` a kind, which is what A16 asked for.

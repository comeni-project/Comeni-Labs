Ragged in, ordered out

The mark is a gate — Comenius's Janua Linguarum Reserata, the biologist's gating step, and the build's own --gate. It is drawn as a change of state across a threshold rather than as an opening, so it says what the tool does instead of merely pointing at a doorway.

Everything below reuses the tokens already in mendel-dashboard.html. No second palette. Type is IBM Plex, which the dashboard spec already names for production.

A1 × B3, lighter

B3's weight came from two filled outline rectangles plus four band lines stacked in the middle. These keep the book reading but pay far less ink for it: a spine is legible from two edges and two bands, which is four thin strokes instead of a block. All three are A1's stroke field with the threshold rebuilt.

D1 · one spine

The bar becomes a single spine — two edges, a band top and bottom, and an open middle. That opening is the gate, and it costs nothing extra: it's the part of the spine that isn't drawn.

D2 · two posts

B3's idea at a fraction of the weight: the posts are lines with bands rather than blocks. Widest opening of the three, so the gate reads hardest, and the traffic genuinely passes between two books.

D3 · A1 + bands

A1 with nothing removed and two bands added, so the bar reads as a spine seen head-on. The smallest possible change to the one you liked — and the only one that keeps all five strokes.

Shortlist — the gate and the book are one object

A spine is a threshold: the thing you pass through by reading. So the two directions collapse into one, which is why these four sit together. Everything below this section is the working history that led here — kept, not current.

B1 · open book

Built on straight lines and mitred corners so it matches the 3px, instrument-like system rather than fighting it. The channel between the page blocks is the spine and the opening at once.

B2 · passage

The channel widened until it reads as a way through, with one line running the whole span — ragged into the gate, resolved out of it. The gate reading is strongest here.

B3 · gateposts

Two book spines standing as the posts, headbands and all, with the traffic passing between them. The most literal statement of the whole idea: books are the gate.

C1 · curved book

The first pass, kept for comparison. Warmer and more conventionally book-shaped; the curves are the part that sits least well next to the rest of the system.

Four readings of the same idea

All four are the same sentence: uneven goes in, even comes out. They differ in which lab image they borrow to say it.

A1 · threshold

Ragged starts on the left, flush ends on the right. The most abstract and the most durable — it borrows no single technique, so it doesn't date.

A2 · pileup

Stacked reads collapsing to one consensus line. Instantly nameable by anyone who has opened a genome browser. Narrower: it reads as sequencing, not as the whole umbrella.

A3 · tiered

A1 with the four resolution tiers on the exits — solid, faded, dashed, gapped. The product claim compressed into the mark. Loses the dashes below ~24px.

A4 · lane

The same gate turned vertical, so flow runs top to bottom like a gel lane. Squarest silhouette; best in a circular avatar crop.

Concrete

Three marks that are a recognisable thing rather than a diagram. Note what happens: the more nameable a mark gets, the less of it is yours. C1 keeps A1's logic intact — same ragged left, same flush right, same centre threshold — and just makes the threshold a spine. It's the only one here that becomes concrete without giving anything up.

C1 · book

A1 with the bar redrawn as a book spine. Ragged notes on the left page, resolved lines on the right. Comenius wrote the first picture textbook, so this is his object, not a generic one — and the spine is a gate you pass through by reading.

C2 · gel

Two lanes: one smeared, one resolved into clean bands. Unmistakably wet lab, and every user has run one. Narrow — it names a technique, and it's the technique your users are moving away from.

C3 · flask

The honest baseline. Instantly legible as "science", owned by nobody, and therefore owned by nobody in particular. Included so the cost of concreteness is visible rather than asserted.

Making it read as a gate

A1's fault: the bar is solid, so it's a wall. Everything crosses it and nothing passes through an opening. These three break it open. The strokes are unchanged — only the threshold is redrawn, and it changes what the mark says: not everything gets tidied but the gate selects, and what clears it comes out ordered. That is gating in the flow-cytometry sense, and it is also what the router actually does.

A5 · posts

Two posts, an opening between them. The outer two strokes hit a post and stop; the three that clear the gap emerge flush. Closest to A1, so it keeps what you liked.

A6 · jambs

The posts get a lintel and a sill, so the opening is visibly built rather than a break in a line. Most unambiguously a gate; also the most architectural, which costs a little of the instrument feel.

A7 · narrow gate

The opening is tighter than the traffic: four go in, two come out. Says selection hardest, and reads honestly — a gate that lets everything through isn't deciding anything.

A1 · for comparison

The wall. Included so the difference is visible side by side rather than described.

Small sizes

Where identities die. A1 and A4 survive to 16px; A3 does not — its dashes fill in. If you want the tier language, A3 is a large-format variant of A1, not a replacement.

Logotype

IBM Plex Serif, tracked to -0.025em. The serif is the dashboard spec's existing call and the reason to keep it holds here: scholarly, not startup. Set in sentence case — an all-caps wordmark would fight the mark's horizontal strokes.

Combination lockups

The rule: mark + Comeni + a small sans qualifier. Comeni is always the serif word; the child name is always the uppercase sans tag. That is what keeps the org reading as an umbrella and Labs, Code and Registry reading as equals under it.

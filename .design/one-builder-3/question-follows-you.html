My recommendation: the open question follows you

This combines A and C. While the question is the latest thing in the chat, it's a full card (A). Once you chat past it, it shrinks into a compact bar above the text box (C) until you answer it. Left: when it's asked. Right: after a side question.

wait, what's the difference between a GTF and a GFF?
Both describe where genes are on the genome. GTF is a stricter flavour of GFF that most RNA-seq tools, STAR included, expect. If your annotation is a GFF3, I can convert it as a step. You don't need to decide that now.
ok and do I need ERCC spike-ins?
Only if your lab added them to the samples. They're a wet-lab choice, not something the pipeline can add. If you did, say so and I'll account for them.

Why this is best for a researcher

  • You learn where you're asked. The first time you see a question, it carries the full explanation and the answer notes, which is the "tutor" part.
  • Curiosity is never punished. Ask about GFFs, spike-ins, anything; the open question is still one click away and never scrolls off. It's never silently skipped (an anti-goal in the design).
  • The chat still reads like a chat. No stack of boxes, because only the current question is ever a full card.
  • It matches the queue choice. A message waiting in line and the docked question sit together at the bottom: what's still open is always where you're typing.
  • On a phone the dock is one line ("Still needed: which mouse genome? ›") and expands on tap, so it doesn't eat the screen.
